Exercise 2.1: Safety Vocabulary:

Loss: An unacceptable outcome involving harm to people, property, or the environment.  
Example: A collision with a pedestrian causing injury.

Hazard: A system state that, under worst-case environmental conditions, can lead to a loss.  
Example: The vehicle is moving at high speed while a pedestrian is in its direct path.

Risk: The combination of the likelihood of a hazard occurring and the severity of the resulting loss.  
Example: A "High Risk" scenario is a vehicle driving at 50 km/h in a crowded school zone with faulty brakes.

Exercise 2.3: Losses: 

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ID  | Loss Description                               | Why Unacceptable
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
L-1 | Death or injury to persons                     | Protection of human life is the primary safety constraint and a legal/ethical requirement 
L-2 | Damage to the ego-vehicle or external objects  | Leads to high financial costs, liability issues, and loss of mission capability
L-3 | Damage to the environment                      | Results in environmental harm or public utility disruption
L-4 | Loss of mission/transportation                 | The system fails to provide its intended utility to the user
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Exercise 2.6: Unsafe Control Actions (UCAs): 

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ID     | Controller         | Control Action       | UCA Type           | Hazard(s)     | Unsafe Scenario
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
UCA-1  | Planning Module    | Brake                | Not provided       | H-1           | The planner fails to issue a brake command when a pedestrian is detected in the path.
UCA-2  | Planning Module    | Accelerate           | Provided unsafely  | H-1, H-2      | The planner issues an acceleration command while a vehicle is stopped directly ahead.
UCA-3  | Planning Module    | Steer                | Wrong timing       | H-1, H-5      | The planner initiates a lane change maneuver too late to avoid a stationary obstacle.
UCA-4  | Human Operator     | Override/Takeover    | Not provided       | H-1, H-3      | The human fails to intervene when the system enters an ODD violation (e.g., heavy fog).
UCA-5  | Human Operator     | Deactivate Autonomy  | Provided unsafely  | H-5           | The human deactivates the system during a high-speed curve without taking steering control.
UCA-6  | Planning Module    | Brake                | Wrong duration     | H-5           | The planner applies full emergency braking but releases it before the vehicle has fully stopped.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Exercise 2.7: Safety Constraints:

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
UCA   | Safety Constraint                                                                                                       | Level        | Verification
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
UCA-1 | The Planning Module must receive a brake signal if the distance oracle/perception model identifies an object within 2m  | Model-level  | Performance testing of the distance oracle and perception labels.
UCA-2 | The Planning Module must inhibit acceleration commands if the distance to the leading vehicle is decreasing below a safety threshold | Model-level | Evaluating the collision-avoidance logic against edge-case datasets.
UCA-4 | The system must provide a clear, multimodal alert (visual/auditory) to the operator within 500ms of detecting an ODD violation | System-level | Human-in-the-loop simulation and latency testing of the notification pipeline.
UCA-5 | The system must prevent deactivation of steering/braking control until it detects that the operator's hands are on the wheel and feet are on the pedals | System-level | Functional safety testing of pressure/capacitive sensors and driver monitoring logic.
UCA-6 | Emergency braking must be sustained until the vehicle velocity reaches 0 km/h or the obstacle is no longer in the trajectory | Model-level | Verification of the Planner's state machine logic in diverse simulation scenarios.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Exercise 2.8: Causal Loss Scenarios:

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
UCA    | Causal Scenario	                                                                   | Root Cause	                                    | Related Constraint
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
UCA-1  | Perception model fails to detect a pedestrian in low-light/high-glare conditions | Incorrect Feedback: The model provides a "clear path" label despite an obstacle being present | Model-level: Robustness to lighting variations.  

UCA-2  | The Planner receives a "0m" distance from the Distance Oracle but treats it as a sensor error and ignores it | Flawed Internal Model: The controller misinterprets valid sensor data as noise | Model-level: Collision-avoidance logic.  

UCA-4  | The human operator is distracted by a mobile device and misses the takeover alert | Human Factor: Attention lapse leads to a failure to respond to a system-initiated handoff | System-level: Multimodal alerts and monitoring.  

UCA-6  | A high-latency connection between the perception model and the planner causes the "obstacle cleared" signal to arrive late | Timing Delay: Stale information causes the controller to stop the safety action prematurely | Model-level: State machine timing verification.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
